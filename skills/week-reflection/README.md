# week-reflection

A short, guided reflection on one strategic project decision from your week. You look at what you decided, what else you considered, what you expected and what you actually saw. The conversation ends with a next step you choose yourself. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) gives the reasons and sources for each choice.

It doesn't activate for status updates, planning next week's tasks, performance reviews, team retrospectives or therapy.

## How it works

1. **One decision.** You pick one project decision from the week, not the whole week.
2. **One question per turn.** The coach asks one question, then waits. No lists, no forms, no multiple-choice menu.
3. **Alternatives before the outcome.** You reconstruct what else was on the table and what you expected before you look at what happened. That keeps the outcome from making the decision look more predictable than it was.
4. **Observed effect, not just a feeling.** The coach asks what you actually saw: an event, a number, a comment or a change in the work.
5. **You choose the next step.** The coach never picks it for you. It repeats your step in your words and asks nothing more.

## Example

This conversation is fictional. It shows the shape, not a real project.

> **Coach:** Which decision from this week do you want to look at?
>
> **You:** I turned down a branding project to keep a week free for a retainer client.
>
> **Coach:** What else did you consider at the time?
>
> **You:** Taking it and subcontracting the production, or offering a smaller scope.
>
> **Coach:** What did you expect each option to lead to?
>
> **You:** I expected the retainer client to use the free week for a new landing page, and the smaller scope to protect cash flow without using my time.
>
> **Coach:** What did you actually see happen?
>
> **You:** The retainer client postponed the work. I spent two days on admin, and the branding project went to another studio.
>
> **Coach:** Where did that differ from what you expected?
>
> **You:** I assumed the retainer would move fast. I never asked them to confirm the week.
>
> **Coach:** What would you do differently if you faced the same choice again?
>
> **You:** I would ask the retainer client for the dates before I reserve a week for them.
>
> **Coach:** What is the next step you choose for next week?
>
> **You:** I will send them a short message on Monday asking for the dates. That is the step I choose.

## What it needs

Nothing extra. It works in any chat that can hold a conversation. No web search, files or storage are needed.

## Opinions you may not share

This skill takes positions. Change them in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) if they don't suit you.

- One decision per conversation.
- One question per turn, with no lists of questions.
- Alternatives and expectations come before the observed effect.
- The user chooses the next step, not the coach.
- The coach closes after the choice and does not add a plan.
- Public examples are fictional.
- In Dutch, the coach says *je*, not *u*.
