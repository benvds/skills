# Craft: the floor

The floor is what every build must reach, whatever the direction. It never picks the direction. A pinned brief or the committed direction overrides anything here; your own habit does not. With the floor met, spend the effort on the idea.

Numbers marked *study* come from Taste Skill's study of gallery sites, where top means a model-panel score of 7.5 or higher and weak means under 6.5. Unmarked numbers are common craft practice.

## Type

- **Few sizes, far apart.** Two or three sizes do the visible work: one large, one small, little in between. Five sizes is the ceiling. On strong sites the largest text is bigger than on weak ones (median 8.4vw against 5.0vw, *study*); weak work clusters in the middle.
- **Display:** tracking between -0.01 and -0.04em, leading 0.9 to 1.05. Headlines of about five words, two lines or fewer at every width.
- **Text:** 16 to 20px on screen, leading 1.4 to 1.6, 45 to 75 characters per line, in the full ink colour.
- **One family is normal, two is the limit.** Choose each face like an object from the subject's world, and write down why it belongs. On Operate and Read surfaces a good workhorse or system face is a fine choice. On Persuade and Experience surfaces, a face with a point of view.
- **Left-aligned by default.** Centre one moment at most.
- Tabular figures wherever numbers are compared. Balanced line breaks in headings.

## Colour

- **Pick a strategy before colours:** restrained, committed, full palette or drenched (see SKILL.md). Commit at the scale of the whole surface: fields that own regions, not accents sprinkled over a neutral ground.
- **One ground, one ink, one accent** is the usual base. The accent marks the action and perhaps one more thing.
- **Tint your neutrals** from the palette instead of pure grey. On a coloured surface, tint secondary text from that hue, never grey.
- **Separate surfaces by a step in tone,** not by a line plus a shadow. Choose one way to show elevation.
- **Contrast:** body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1.
- **Light or dark** follows from the physical scene: who uses this, where, in what light.

## Space

- **One grid and one pair of equal outer margins,** used everywhere. Asymmetry lives inside the grid, not in the frame.
- **Tight groups, generous separation.** The gap between sections is clearly larger than any gap inside one. More space above a heading than below it.
- **Two or three left edges** for text, reused on purpose.
- **No accidental voids.** An empty half beside a short column is a hole. One deliberate rest per page or deck is enough.

## Pictures

- **One dominant picture beats six small ones.** On top sites the hero picture covers at least half the first view 68% of the time, against 48% on weak sites (*study*).
- **The order of preference:** the project's own material; generated, if you can; real pictures of the subject's world from open collections (Wikimedia Commons, Openverse, museum open-access collections), with a licence that allows the use and a credit; drawings made for this subject in one consistent style; type and colour alone. Look at each picture before you use it.
- **Same crop ratios, same treatment.** Square corners unless the direction says otherwise.
- **No picture is better than a placeholder,** a stock cliché, or an abstract blob.
- **Software shows its own interface,** built as a real fragment with data that adds up. A fake dashboard of grey bars is the weakest picture there is.

## Motion

- **One authored moment,** not scattered effects and not the same entrance on every section.
- Ease out (exponential or quintic), roughly 200 to 500ms. No bounce.
- Content is visible by default; motion never hides it if the script fails.
- Every move has a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content.

## States and the parts you didn't draw

- Hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, empty and error, all designed.
- Text selection, focus rings, scrollbars, link underlines and form controls carry the design too. Browser defaults are the cheapest sign that a page was assembled, not built.
- Controls name their action. Errors name the problem and the way out.
- Real links and buttons, alt text, keyboard focus, a small view that is its own composition.

## Per medium

**Web page.** The first view is a thesis, not a header: show the subject doing its job. A clear title in the first view and a heading on every section. The footer is a designed close, not a sitemap. Consider one live thing the visitor can do with the subject's real content, which works with a thumb and has a plain fallback.

**App screen.** Quiet panels, aligned figures, colour spent on data and state, not on decoration. Familiar patterns where users expect them; character in the details: type, spacing, the empty states, the one moment of feedback. Real-looking data that adds up, and the overlong, empty and error versions of every list.

**Slides.** One message per slide, written as a sentence. On the slide only what the audience must see; the rest goes in the speaker notes. Type large enough for the back row. The deck has one visual system and one recurring signature, and the closing slide belongs to it.

**Document.** Structure for the reader's question: headings that say something, a short first paragraph with the point, tables where things are compared. A measure of 60 to 75 characters, generous leading, one text face. Bold only what a skimming reader must not miss.

**Poster or single image.** One dominant element, readable from across the room. The name or the date at the second size, never only in small print.
