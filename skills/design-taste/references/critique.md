# Critique: judging what you see

Judge the rendered result, never the code or the plan. You made it, so you will read your intentions into anything that is not a picture. Screenshots show only what is there.

Take screenshots at the sizes the artefact ships at: desktop and phone width for a page or screen, the slide size for a deck, a page spread for a document. Settle or switch off entrance motion first, or hidden elements look missing. Open every screenshot before you judge.

## The passes

Make each pass on its own. Don't blend them.

1. **One second, then five.** Glance and look away. What did you see? There should be one answer: a word, an image, an object. "A website" or "a slide" means no focal point. Then look for five seconds as a stranger: what is this, who is it for, what do I do next?
2. **Squint.** Blur until text turns into grey bars. The view should reduce to two or three large masses with clear size differences. Many similar small masses mean everything is medium and nothing leads.
3. **Cover the name.** Could this belong to a competitor, or to another industry? If swapping the name and the noun gives an equally valid result, there is no idea. Then check the rest: does the idea reach the last section, the closing slide, the empty state, the footer?
4. **Edges.** Follow the left edge of every text block down the view. Count the distinct positions. A composed view has two or three, reused on purpose. Six means things were placed one at a time.
5. **Space and frame.** Are related things close and unrelated things far? Is the gap between sections clearly larger than any gap inside one? Are the outer margins equal? Is any area empty by accident (a hole) rather than by decision (a rest)? A hole usually sits beside a short column or where a decoration was removed.
6. **Type.** Is the jump from body to headline decisive, three times or more? More than five sizes? A headline with one word alone on its last line? Lines over about 75 characters, or centred text longer than three lines?
7. **Colour.** Name the proportions: which colour holds most of the surface, which supports, where the accent is. An accent in ten places means nothing. One on the single action means "here". Is running text in full ink, or faint grey?
8. **Pictures.** Is every picture doing a job: showing the thing, setting the place, proving the claim? Do crops share ratios and treatment? Any grey box, stretched photo, or fake screen made of bars?
9. **Sameness.** Write one word for the layout of each section or slide: split, list, full-bleed, grid, statement. The same word twice in a row, or three times in total, is the template showing.
10. **Phone, or the small view.** Is the small view a composition of its own, or the large one squeezed? Does anything run off the edge?
11. **Fullness and substance.** Is each screen a composed whole, or a narrow column with dead ground on either side? Does every part the brief asked for deliver its information completely, at a readable size? A clean view that is thin loses to a richer one with flaws.
12. **Collisions and states.** Does anything lie over a headline or a button? On interactive work: hover, focus, loading, empty, error, and overlong content.
13. **Mode.** Persuade: does a first-time visitor know what this is and what to do within seconds? Operate: can a regular user scan, compare and act without hunting? Read: can a reader find their question and follow the argument? Experience: does the work lead?

## The score

Score four criteria from 1 to 10. Adjust the weights to the mode.

| Criterion | Persuade / Experience | Operate / Read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design: composition, type, colour, imagery, craft | 40% | 35% |
| Usability: orientation, readability, obvious actions | 30% | 45% |
| Creativity: an idea you can state in a sentence, carried through | 20% | 10% |
| Content: specific, true, edited | 10% | 10% |

Anchors:
- **9–10:** every view holds; nothing could be moved or removed. Rare.
- **8:** confident and coherent; one or two soft spots a designer would notice.
- **7:** clean and professional, a clear system, little tension.
- **6:** tidy but anonymous. Reads as a kit.
- **5 or less:** weak hierarchy, accidental spacing, default type, mismatched pictures.

If your first score for your own work is 9, score it again as the juror who did not build it.

**Caps** override the arithmetic:
- Pass 3 fails (it could belong to anyone): creativity at most 6.
- Pass 1 fails (no clear answer to "what is this" in five seconds): usability at most 6.
- A part the brief asked for is missing or reduced to a few words: content at most 6.
- A placeholder (grey box, lorem ipsum, "image here"): content at most 5.
- Anything covering text or an action, or content cut off: usability at most 5.

## Writing the critique

Write it down in this shape, so rounds can be compared:

```
ROUND n
Seen in one second: …
Idea (one sentence): …
Layout words by section: …
Scores: design _ · usability _ · creativity _ · content _ → overall _._
Caps applied: …
Top three problems, most visible first, each with where it shows and the fix:
1. …
2. …
3. …
```

Name problems the way a designer would to a colleague: "the supporting paragraph starts 40px left of the headline", "this is the third split layout in a row", "the phone crop cuts the bottle out of frame".

## What to fix first

Fix in this order. Later items cannot rescue earlier ones.

1. **The idea.** If pass 3 fails, change the concept, not the details.
2. **Hierarchy.** One dominant element per view. Make the big thing bigger and the small things fewer.
3. **Composition.** Shared edges, spacing, variety between sections, holes.
4. **Type.** Scale contrast, line breaks, measure, leading.
5. **Pictures.** Replace weak ones, unify crops and treatment.
6. **Colour.** Proportion and accent discipline.
7. **Detail.** States, focus, small alignments, the small view.
8. **Motion.** Last.

When a fix removes something, don't fill the gap with another ornament. Close the gap, enlarge what remains, or let a picture or a colour field take the space.

## When to stop

Two rounds, three at most: critique, fix the top three, render, critique. Stop early when a round finds nothing a stranger would notice. If the last round still scores under 7.5, say so plainly with the remaining problems. Don't round up.
