# Craft: what the coach checks against

These are the rules the coach uses for feedback on the point, the outline and the draft. They come from the overview article "Hoe schrijf je een goed artikel?" (October 2026) and its sources. Each rule says whether it rests on research or is a writing rule from practice. Don't present a practice rule as a research finding.

Most writing research was done with school pupils and students, and most persuasion research with health messages and online panels. Effects are mostly small to medium. Say so when it matters for the advice.

## Point and reader

- **One point, one sentence.** Everything in the text serves it. Practice rule. Writing about a subject also helps the writer understand it (meta-analysis, 56 experiments, effect size 0.30, school pupils) [Graham, Kiuhara & MacKay 2020].
- **One concrete reader.** Experts forget what it is like not to know (the curse of knowledge) [Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber 1989]. Ask: what does this reader already know, want to know, and think now? Test the first paragraph on someone from the audience.
- **Planning first.** Writing a plan before the draft improved text quality in experiments with students [Kellogg 1987; 1988]. Prewriting activities and goals gave small to medium gains in school studies [Graham, Collins & Ciullo 2024].

## Informative text

- **Core first.** The point in the first paragraph. Practice rule.
- **Visible structure.** Headings and other signals improve remembering of learning material (meta-analyses; one found d = 0.38) [Schneider et al. 2018; Alpizar et al. 2020]. Headings say what is in the section.
- **One idea per paragraph, core in the first sentence.** The first sentences alone should show the argument. Practice rule.
- **Connectives.** In a Dutch study with 794 adolescents, contrastive and causal connectives ("maar", "daarom") improved comprehension and additive ones ("en", "bovendien") lowered it [Kleijn, Pander Maat & Sanders 2019]. Show why and against where the reader wouldn't see it; don't glue sentences with "and".

## Opinion piece

- **A claim someone can contradict.** Practice rule.
- **Story or numbers.** When the reader is personally involved, a story persuades more; when involvement is low, numbers do [Freling et al. 2020, 61 studies]. Numbers move beliefs and attitudes more; stories move intentions more [Zebregs et al. 2015]. Mostly health communication.
- **The strongest objection, answered.** Two-sided messages that refute the other side persuade more than one-sided ones; two-sided messages that only mention the other side persuade less [O'Keefe 1999, 107 studies]. Average effects of message choices are small [O'Keefe 2021].
- **The reader's values.** Arguments reframed to the values of the audience persuaded more in six US studies on political issues [Feinberg & Willer 2015]. Use it only if the writer believes it.

## Sentences and words

- **Readability in Dutch.** Comprehension is predicted by word frequency, concrete words, clause length and the distance between words that belong together (LiNT, 120 texts, 2700 pupils) [Pander Maat, Kleijn & Frissen 2023]. Common, concrete words; short clauses; no tangconstructies.
- **No needless long words.** Readers rated writers of needlessly complex text as less intelligent (five experiments, US students, English) [Oppenheimer 2006].
- **Errors cost trust.** Readers judge content more negatively when it has spelling and grammar errors [cited in Graham, Collins & Ciullo 2024]. Check errors last.
- **Actor as subject, action as verb.** Practice rule; the `plain-writing` skill has the details.

## Uncertainty

- **Say how sure you are, in numbers where possible.** Stating uncertainty lowered trust only a little, mostly when it was stated in words rather than as a numeric range (five experiments, 5,780 participants) [van der Bles et al. 2020]. Say once where it matters; don't hedge every sentence.

## Revising and practising

- **Writing more is not enough.** In school studies, extra writing time alone gave effects too small to matter; goals, feedback, peer help, model texts and strategy instruction did help [Graham, Collins & Ciullo 2024].
- **Revise in rounds with one focus each,** and let a reader say where they lost interest. Practice rule.

## AI in the writing process

- **Speed.** Professionals with ChatGPT finished short writing tasks 40% faster with 18% higher rated quality; facts were not checked [Noy & Zhang 2023].
- **Learning.** Students with unrestricted GPT-4 access did worse afterwards without it; a version that gave hints instead of answers largely prevented that (mathematics) [Bastani et al. 2025].
- **Sameness.** Stories written with AI ideas were rated as more creative but were more alike [Doshi & Hauser 2024].
- **Ownership.** People felt less ownership of AI-generated text, and more when they had more influence on it [Draxler et al. 2024].
- **Made-up sources.** GPT-3.5 fabricated 55% and GPT-4 18% of the references in generated literature reviews [Walters & Wilder 2023].

That is why the coach has these rules: the user makes the first attempt at every part, proposals by the coach are marked, and every source is opened before it is cited.
